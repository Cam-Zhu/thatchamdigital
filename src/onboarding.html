Step one

Everything I need, in one go.

This is the only long bit. Fill it in once and I've got what I need to build the whole thing. So there's no back and forth, no chasing you for your opening hours three weeks from now.

Rough answers are fine. I'll tidy up the wording. If you genuinely don't know something, write "not sure" and we'll sort it on a call.

Takes about 10 minutes. It doesn't save as you go, so if you're interrupted you'll lose it.

1. You and the business

Exactly as you want it written on the site, including any Ltd or & Sons.
As if you were telling someone at the school gate. "I fix bikes" beats "bicycle maintenance solutions".
Old website, Facebook page, Instagram, a Checkatrade listing — paste whatever links you've got.

2. Who you want through the door

Think of a real one you've had. What were they like, what did they need?
Towns and villages you cover, or how far you'll travel. This feeds straight into how you show up in local searches.
The honest answer. This usually becomes the headline.

3. What you sell

One per line. Prices are optional but they work. People are far more likely to get in touch when they've got a rough idea what it'll cost. "From £45" is enough.
Or "by appointment", or "evenings and weekends". Whatever's true.
Leave blank if you work from home and would rather not. It's not required, though a town name does help you locally.

4. What should a visitor do?

Pick one. A site that asks for three things gets none of them.
Up to five are included. Most businesses do fine with Home, Services, About, and Contact. Tell me if you're not sure.

5. How it should look

Any industry at all. They don't have to be competitors. A line on what you like about each is worth more than the links on their own.
Colours you hate, a competitor's site that puts you off, sliding banners, pop-ups. Useful to know early.
Have you got a logo? Any colours already in use, uniform or branding?

6. Practical bits

If yes, which one and where did you buy it. If no, tell me what you'd like and I'll check it's free.
A trade show, a season starting, a van getting signwritten. If there's no deadline, say so — that's fine too.

Photos — send these separately

Email them to cameronnrobertson [a] pm.me/a>, or use WeTransfer if there are a lot. Straight off your phone is fine: full size, not resized, and not screenshots.

Worth having: you or your team, your work or premises, and a few finished jobs. Real photos of real work beat stock images every time, even slightly wonky ones.
